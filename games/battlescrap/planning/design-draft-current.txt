Core Mechanics

Tactical starfall-fantasy skirmish. Bid for position, field three starter heroes plus three recruits, activate heroes through a shared action queue, fight with opposed D6 pools, collect Brightstone and complete tactical objectives.

Current revision: starter-test-v11, 21 October 2026. CONFIRMED rules incorporate the latest designer instructions; TEST numbers are playtest defaults, not measured balance. Previous linked hero cards, generic Push/cleanse actions, Boosted, Stunned, Taunt and card-for-AP uses are retired.

Work split: documents, art and the current PnP are authored here under the designer’s latest request. Sol retains digital implementation. No claim is made that the application implements this revision.

Setup and Preparation

Carry forward the existing deployment bidding rules: secret zone choice and nonnegative whole-PP bid; different zones ignore bids and randomly choose first side; same zone gives the higher bidder its chosen zone and first deployment/activation, with the winning bid added to the opponent’s budget. Tied bids receive secret positive increments until unequal. No bid cap.

Field exactly three distinct available heroes and three free recruits. Buy equipment, extra recruits, group upgrades and extra starting draws before seeing cards. Only unused bid compensation survives as an attacker-reroll bank; ordinary leftovers expire. Roster access does not waive fielding cost.

Starting PP is 15. Field exactly three heroes; starter prices span 3–7 PP. No inherited 20/21-PP starting budget. Later campaign stages have higher-cost heroes and higher preparation budgets; progression is OPEN.

Fire, Lightning and Water remain the fixed, printed elements of abilities and tactic cards. Remove element holders, hero-element assignment and matching tableau element tokens entirely. Combat bonuses need no element match; ability payments match the ability’s printed element. A hero does not have a separate assigned element. Restore faction ownership portrait holders only.

Deploy heroes and recruits individually in the chosen zone (north blue or south stone). Never deploy or respawn into partial hexes, including overflow placement. If full, choose a nearest reachable empty hex, choosing ties. Draw a starting tactic hand and one hidden tactical objective per side. Starting hand/deck quantities are TEST below.

Turns and Action Queue

Only heroes have queue cards: six cards for a three-hero-per-side battle. Recruits have no queue entry. The lower bidder owns highest position 5, then ownership alternates down: lower bidder 5/3/1; higher bidder 4/2/0. Shuffle each side’s three portraits and randomly deal into its assigned positions. When equal bids are ignored because zones differ, use the second player for positions 5/3/1 as the test tie fallback; first-player selection otherwise follows existing bidding rules.

At the start of a side’s turn, apply scenario start-turn scoring and immediately check victory. Select any friendly hero, including position 0, capture that position’s AP/RAP entitlement, move its card to position 0 and shift intervening cards. Resolve any hero newly reaching the far end of the queue, including poison; then resolve the selected hero’s start-activation poison. Death and victory interrupt further actions as appropriate.

AP can be spent on the selected hero or any friendly recruit. RAP (recruit action points) can be spent only on friendly recruits. AP and RAP may combine to pay one recruit action; spend RAP first by default, with the expenditure shown. No other friendly hero gets ordinary actions.

Hero basic actions: Move, Attack, Grab, Respawn. Each distinct action has its own use count during that activation: first use costs 1 AP, second 2 AP, third 3 AP, and so on. Move–Attack–Move costs 1 + 1 + 2 AP. Paid unique abilities have their own repeat counter; base 1 AP becomes 1/2/3, base 2 AP becomes 2/3/4. Card-only abilities remain card-only on repeat use. Printed “once” means once per own activation unless another timing is stated.

Each recruit has one shared action counter for the entire friendly turn, across all its actions and regardless of AP versus RAP. Move–Attack–Move by one recruit costs 1 + 2 + 3. Another recruit begins at 1. Counters reset at the next friendly turn, not on respawn; the former once-per-action restriction is removed.

Six-position queue from bottom to top: 0 = 1 AP / 0 RAP; 1 = 1 AP / 1 RAP; 2 = 2 AP / 1 RAP; 3 = 2 AP / 2 RAP; 4 = 3 AP / 2 RAP; 5 = 4 AP / 3 RAP. Every step increases AP or RAP, with neither decreasing. All positions are selectable, including 0. No queue position grants a tactic draw. Selecting slot 0 leaves the order unchanged and does not itself cause a new far-end arrival.

End activation: collect automatic Brightstone pickups and tactic rewards, enforce hand limits; claim at most one eligible tactical objective and resolve its VP/tactic reward, checking victory immediately; if the objective hand is empty, draw one objective; resolve the activated hero’s end-activation ability, if any; discard unspent AP/RAP and pass. No further checkpoint is invented after victory. Future Windlass regional Enraged is gained here only by the activated hero, never all idle heroes.

Movement, Combat and Statuses

Move spends its current action cost to move up to printed Move. Occupancy, board bounds and impassable terrain apply; units cannot move through units. Attack spends its current action cost and resolves one attack. Grab collects a Brightstone on the unit’s own hex immediately; both heroes and recruits may collect. Automatic end-activation collection remains available to all living friendly units standing on stones (TEST timing interpretation). Respawn spends its current action cost, returns one defeated model fully healed and status-free using deployment overflow rules, and does not reset that model’s action counters.

Push and Pull are effects of named abilities, not basic actions. For Push N, move the target up to N adjacent hex steps, each increasing hex distance from the source’s fixed position by exactly 1. For Pull N, each step reduces that distance by exactly 1. The effect’s controller chooses legal steps. Never enter occupied/impassable/out-of-bounds hexes; stop when blocked or defeated. No sideways step at equal distance, no moving through the source, no automatic damage, Dazed or opposed roll. An ability specifies any additional effects. A blocked displacement does not cancel separately printed effects.

Attack and Defense are D6 pool sizes. Roll simultaneously; compare highest face. If both highest faces are 6, compare counts of 6. Attacker wins every remaining tie. Never sum pips or compare other sorted faces. A normal hit deals 1 damage; two more attacker sixes adds 1 damage. All damage bonuses apply only if the attack hits. Two more defender sixes: TEST replacement reward is 1 Fortified, granted after old-token cleanup; Boosted no longer exists.

Defender and attacker alternate pre-roll tactic/pass; a play resets consecutive passes; two passes close responses. Each side modifies only its own participating pool. Combat tactics stack. Minimum final dice pool is 0. At 0, roll two dice and keep only the lower as the entire result; the other die never counts toward sixes. Generic tactic bonuses never require element or holder matching and may help heroes or recruits. Declare damage bonuses before rolling.

Carry-forward TEST heavy attack: halve final Attack dice, rounding up; a hit adds 1 damage and a miss gives the attacker 1 Dazed. This is an attack modifier, not a fifth basic action. Attacker-only rerolls remain 1 PP per own die from banked bid compensation; explicitly finish rerolls before resolution. No automatic counterattack exists; any future printed counterattack follows ordinary combat and cannot chain.

Only four status token types exist. Enraged: +1 damage on the next attack per token, only on a hit; consume the entire participating stack after that attack, including on a miss. Fortified: +1 Defense die per token on the next defense; consume the entire participating stack afterward. Dazed: −1 Defense die per token on the next defense; consume the entire participating stack afterward. Dazed no longer reduces Attack dice. Fortified and Dazed both apply and are both spent even if their numerical effects cancel or the minimum pool applies.

Snapshot applicable status stacks when a roll begins. Consume those stacks after the roll is resolved; tokens gained from that roll’s after-effects survive. Enraged is not spent on defense; Fortified/Dazed are not spent on attack. Statuses belong to the unit, not its queue card position.

Each application of Poison adds the specified token(s) and swaps the affected hero one position toward slot 0 with the hero below it, stopping at slot 0. Poison on a hero deals 1 damage per poison token at BOTH the start of that hero’s activation and whenever its card newly reaches the far end of the queue. It persists after damage. Trigger once per arrival event, not repeatedly while parked at the far end; setup placement is not an arrival. Off-board heroes take no poison damage. Applying poison to a recruit defeats it immediately; recruits do not wait for a queue or activation trigger.

If a poisoned character would be healed, remove its poison instead of healing damage. Interpretation for the rules baseline: remove the entire poison stack and heal zero damage from that healing effect, regardless of healing amount; excess healing does not carry over. A poisoned but unwounded character is a legal healing target. Any separate non-healing effect still resolves. A recruit already defeated by poison cannot be saved retroactively by healing.

Heroes have printed HP 2–5; recruits retain 2 HP. Each red cube represents 1 damage received; reaching HP defeats the model. Healing removes cubes only when poison did not replace that healing. No generic Remove Daze or Cleanse action exists.

Each HERO defeat drops one physical Brightstone at the last hex; secured stones never drop again. A hero defeat draws one tactic for its owner and moves its queue card to position 0; recruit defeats draw no consolation card. Ordinary misses and nonlethal damage draw nothing. Resolve private overflow for the drawing owner even off-turn. Defeat, movement of queue cards, poison arrivals and scenario victory must each resolve once.

Three Fires Scenario and Terrain

Each scenario uses a map plus its own deployment/spawning edges, special rules, VP sources and victory threshold or explicit VP payout. A map can support multiple scenarios and multiple group spawning edges; scenario rules specify which are active. No map-specific victory condition is inferred from artwork.

Three Fires uses the supplied unboxed map, with updated bounds and bonfires. North scores 1 VP at its turn start unless south controls at least two fires; south scores 1 VP if it controls at least two. Control means more units adjacent to that fire than the opponent; ties are uncontrolled. Enemy defeats award 1 VP; friendly fire none. Confirmed target: 8 VP; check immediately after every award.

Structures arrive on later maps, particularly Timberwood. They block movement but never line of sight and are destructible only by abilities that say so. Nothing blocks line of sight; do not add starter-map structures.

Random setup: every map has nine X locations. For this map shuffle four traps and five Brightstone tokens with identical backs face down onto the Xs, then reveal all before play. The boxed reference identifies positions only; print the original map without boxes.

Any movement into a trap deals 1 damage, then removes it. Any movement into fire deals 1 damage; ending a character’s turn there also deals 1 damage. Resolve each entry separately, including voluntary and forced movement, stopping if defeated. Fire hexes are enterable. TEST recruit timing: fire end-turn damage applies to recruits that acted during that friendly turn. Moving an idle hero by an ability is not itself ending its activation.

TEST structure representation: impassable occupied hexes, with adjacency measured normally. Structures have no ordinary HP/attack target status and grant no automatic cover unless a scenario or ability specifies it. Do not inherit old temporary-wall expiry or cover rules onto new structures. Preserve any explicitly authored edge-wall rules separately.

Melee flanking is +1 Attack die PER other friendly unit adjacent to the target that has no adjacent enemy OTHER than that target. Recruits can assist; the attacker cannot count itself. This is the only generic flanking bonus; ranged attacks never gain it.

Ordinary ranged attacks keep their printed range, extended range up to twice printed range at −1 Attack die, and −1 Attack die if any enemy is adjacent to the attacker. Units do not block ranged sight. An EXACTLY 2 attack/ability can target only hex distance 2: no range-1 target, no extended range and no Sighting Lens extension. Non-attack abilities gain no dice/flanking modifiers unless printed.

Elevation remains an optional TEST: Low 0 / High 1; ranged high-to-low attacks gain +1 Attack die. No new climbing/falling/sight rules. Off unless the supplied map enables it. Poison-defeat credit for VP is OPEN; recommended: remember the enemy that applied poison and award defeat VP once to that side, without counting the poison damage as an attack for objectives.

Battle Decks and Card Use

Both sides use the same generic tactic-card set, with separately shuffled personal draw/discard piles as the TEST continuation of the existing setup. Remove all twenty linked hero cards and all prior named tactic effects. Each physical tactic shows Fire, Lightning or Water and either +2 Attack (attack icon) or +2 Defense (shield icon) at the bottom.

Discard a tactic for exactly one use: activate a unique ability whose printed element matches; pay an expressly printed amplification cost; use its printed +2 Attack or +2 Defense dice bonus in that role; OR add +1 Attack die, +1 Defense die, or +1 damage to a hit. Printed +2 and flexible +1 combat uses require NO element/holder match. Cards may stack; one discard never pays two uses. There is no card-for-AP use.

Ability elements are fixed in the text. A base ability normally costs one matching tactic; some powerful abilities also cost AP. An amplification discard is additional to the activation discard and any AP. Card-only abilities never acquire an AP repeat surcharge. These choices make tactic income, hand size and repeated card-only effects important balance variables.

TEST deck proposal: 24 tactics per side, 8 per element, consisting of 4 Attack and 4 Defense cards in each element; starting hand 4, hand limit 8. Six definitions with four copies each. This replaces both old mixed hero/tactic presets. Deck counts and starting hand are proposals for approval, not confirmed values.

Complete each draw and enforce limit privately. Reshuffle personal tactic discard when draw pile is empty; if both are empty, draw nothing. Draw sources are starting hand, purchased starting draws, each Brightstone pickup, each completed objective and hero defeat. No automatic empty-tactic-hand refill is added: the empty-hand draw rule applies to OBJECTIVES.

Brightstone and Tactical Objectives

For this first test, the shared objective deck has nine distinct cards, to01–to09, printed on one sheet. Every tactical objective awards exactly 1 VP and 1 tactic. Trade Blows (to10) is retained for later testing, also worth 1 VP + 1 tactic, but is not in this test deck. Deal one hidden objective per side; maximum hand 2, next-own-activation eligibility, at most one claim per activation. At friendly turn end, draw one if the hand is empty and the deck is not. Never refill or reshuffle the objective deck. Approximately 20 objectives remain the future target.

Every completed objective gives its printed 1 VP AND one tactic card. It gives no RP, no physical stone and no additional objective draw except the empty-hand rule. Check scenario victory immediately when VP is awarded; do not run a later normal cleanup if victory ends play.

All objective rewards are 1 VP + 1 tactic. Definitions: to01 Draw Blood (hit an enemy) 1 VP; to02 Stand Fast (block an enemy attack) 1 VP; to03 Hero Hunter (defeat an enemy hero with an attack) 1 VP; to04 Break the Rank (defeat two distinct recruits with attacks) 1 VP; to05 Cut Down (defeat a recruit with an attack) 1 VP; to06 Press the Advantage (same hero hits twice this activation) 1 VP; to07 Against the Odds (block an attack whose final pool exceeds your Defense pool) 1 VP; to08 Crushing Blow (deal at least 2 actual damage in an attack) 1 VP; to09 Hold Together (two distinct friendly defenders each block an attack) 1 VP; to10 Trade Blows (same surviving hero both hits and blocks) 1 VP.

Progress counts only attacks resolved while held and eligible, including off-turn defenses and explicit counterattacks. Push/Pull alone, poison damage, terrain damage and draws are not attacks. Break the Rank counts each recruit identity once even after respawn. Trade Blows requires its hero alive at claim. Against the Odds uses final rolled pools after all modifiers/heavy. Crushing Blow uses actual damage after prevention. Other progress persists while held.

Brightstone pickup by a hero OR recruit removes the physical stone, records one secured stone and immediately draws one tactic. It does not draw an objective or give VP. Each secured stone gives 2 RP at game end, once, tentative for campaign balance. Repeated hero respawn/defeat creates new stones; recruit defeat does not. Hero farming remains a playtest risk.

The objective deck never refills or reshuffles. When empty, the empty-hand draw produces no card. Held objectives may still be completed.

These inherited base/victory rewards are not newly approved campaign balance. Catch-up RP will likely depend on the opponent’s campaign-win LEAD; formula, timing and interaction with the victory bonus remain OPEN. Do not implement a guessed catch-up amount.

World and Regions

Magic belongs to everyone—but who gets to decide how it is used?

About thirty years ago, stars fell across Surlune, a world that had never known magic. They landed in orchards, harbors, villages and mountains. Civilization endured; people still farm, trade, build and celebrate. Magic brought new possibilities, unfamiliar creatures and a struggle over who should control them. The world is experiencing its first miracles.

Brightstone is the common name for the fallen material. Small wonders are possible in ordinary hands; rare stonebinders can awaken its greater power and equip a company with it. A stone can irrigate a field, help build a bridge, guide a ship or empower a fighter. Each stone consumed in war could have been somebody’s miracle. Moon-touched birth conditions are not required canon.

The land teaches the magic what to become. Stones in orchards learn growth; stones among bells learn resonance; stones on open plains learn motion. Over decades, local crafts, landscapes and living things develop distinctive wonders. This explains regional heroes and varied abilities without requiring a destroyed precursor civilization.

The Concord promises safety above all. Its leaders claim that starfall is making the world wilder and more dangerous: untamed magic corrupts the land, threatens ordinary lives and could undo generations of progress. In their view, civilized society—shared law, protected settlements, disciplined stonecraft and common institutions—is the only barrier against that future. They offer shelter, roads and dependable protection in exchange for obedience. Their fear is sincere, but protection can become annexation and the defense of progress can justify suppressing unfamiliar ways of life. Their claim that wild magic is inherently corrupting is a faction belief, not an established truth of the setting.

The Wildmoon chooses freedom over safety. Proud, tough and fiercely independent, its wandering clans and free communities meet the changing world with audacity, revelry and a mystical reverence for the moon and fallen stars. Their traditions include moonlit gatherings, celestial omens, oath-swearing and roaming warbands; magic is a wild inheritance to live alongside, not a force civilization is entitled to tame. They swear allegiance to a leader who will defend their right to govern themselves, not rule every aspect of their lives. Loyalty is personal and rests on that promise of self-rule. They accept hardship and danger rather than surrender their independence; their freedom can also make collective restraint and mutual protection difficult. Neither faction has a mandatory universal faction ability.

Seven home regions — 21 October 2026

Windlands — Tall-grass plains, proud clan leaders and prized lion-shifters. Brown, green and red grass-woven dress; Bantu-inspired fictional cultures. Ambush, speed and misdirection.

Highholm — Frozen peaks, frost giants, heavy furs and leather. Longhouses ring with feasts of mead and meat. Norse-inspired warriors turn stone-hard to endure.

Cinder’s Rim — A mysterious volcanic settlement of masked fire-priests and many fire gods. Carved tiki-inspired forms, ember rituals, magma and dangerous bursts of power.

Timberwood — Powerfully built, beardless woodland elves: axe-workers, growers and master joiners. Living public avenues meet towering timber halls. Hardwood arms and armor draw on medieval Japanese forms; every harvest is replanted.

Port Windlass — Starfall whipped the island seas into a frenzy. A few sailors travel behind tamed whales or sharks; many now raid the rivers. Age-of-sail and Polynesian maritime influences meet wind-calling warriors.

These are fictional microcultures with the supplied broad visual influences; neither a region nor a real-world cultural influence determines allegiance. The Concord promises safety through shared institutions; Wildmoon promises freedom and self-rule. Both can recruit from all seven regions. Wellington — Brightstone-contaminated wells created dependence on its buffs. Phalexia — Brightstone fell during an army’s last stand, binding its survivors into mutually supporting formations.

Each player leads a stonebinder’s traveling company serving one of these movements. Both pursue the same deposits, routes and communities. Lesser star showers keep creating opportunities. A regional victory can secure access and earn local trust without ending the wider struggle.

Heroes fight for their homes, livelihoods and ambitions. Any regional hero may join either side: the same orchard keeper might seek Concord irrigation works in one campaign and resist its claims to the valley in another. Winning a battle gives first recruitment choice from that region’s shared pool; subsequent choices create different available rosters in different campaigns.

Hero defeat releases one recoverable Brightstone; recruit defeat releases none. Heroes and recruits secure stones immediately into their side’s reserve. Each physical pickup draws one tactic and pays 2 RP at game end (tentative campaign balance). Completing an objective awards 1 VP and one tactic, without adding a stone. Magical recall remains optional flavor for respawning.

Ownership uses Concord maroon angular-sun and Wildmoon sky-blue antler-crescent symbols. Restore ownership-only portrait holders; never assign elements to holders. Portrait backgrounds express character and region, not ownership. No tableau element tokens.

Hero Ability Resolution

Use the current roster’s one unique ability per starter; no legacy passive or linked cards.

All options use Fire, Lightning or Water as fixed printed elements. “Water” as a cost means discard one Water tactic, not own a Water holder. If an option says “1 AP + Water,” pay its current scaled AP cost and discard one Water tactic. Repeats of a card-only option require another matching card but no AP.

Default timing is the active hero’s own activation outside combat. Options do not create free defensive reactions. An attack granted inside an ability enters normal combat; it is not a basic Attack for equipment or basic-Attack counters. This separate-counter interpretation is TEST and can let players alternate basic and ability attacks cheaply; evaluate combined Attack counting as a possible alternative.

Movement granted within an ability costs no additional AP. TEST: for recruits it increments that model’s shared action-use counter by one per granted Move, so later paid actions reflect activity; it does not charge retroactive AP/RAP. Forced Push/Pull and swaps do not increment action counters. A multi-target ability selects distinct units and resolves them one at a time; skip optional movement explicitly.

Demea can select any two distinct other friendly units on the board, with no targeting range cap, and Pull each up to two legal steps toward her. Ilex moves a currently adjacent ally up to three, then herself up to three; no end-adjacency requirement. Perrin’s aura does not stack through repeated activation; his Enraged tokens do. Hester’s Water discard costs no AP and gives exactly two Fortified.

“Within N” includes distance 0 for allies unless “another” is stated. Enemies are living on-board units. Healing can target wounded or poisoned units; poison replacement removes the full poison stack and heals no damage. A combined heal/buff can target an unwounded unpoisoned ally only if the buff can apply. Never heal beyond printed HP.

Taunt and Taunt tokens are removed. Future Highholm uses Pull effects and bad statuses on adjacent enemies that move away; exact future ability text is not yet locked.

The three-nearest-enemies abilities select all available enemies up to three within the printed radius, with the acting player choosing distance ties. They cannot skip a nearer enemy to reach a farther priority target. Resolve the chosen list from the initial positions; later movement does not retarget.

EXACTLY 2 is a hard targeting condition measured when an attack/effect begins. If a chained Push fails to leave the target at distance 2, a subsequent exact-range attack cannot occur; the paid ability is not refunded. Timed effects do not stack with another copy of themselves unless stated; status tokens do stack.

Regional abilities do not belong to starters. Later recruited heroes have their region’s ability as well as their unique.

Hero Costing Heuristic — TEST

Starting PP: 15. Exactly three heroes. Starter prices: integer 3–7; the strongest package should substantially outperform the cheapest, without treating point ratio as a proved win-rate ratio.

Evaluate durability from HP and survival under opposed dice; offense from hit probability times damage over a normal activation; mobility/control from reachable targets, displacement and hazard opportunities; support from affected allies. Weight ability frequency by matching-card supply and escalating AP. Estimate synergy separately rather than pricing each printed die identically.

Map the weakest starter package to 3 and strongest to 7. Preserve half-point intermediate estimates, then round once. Current hand-set prices are in the roster, not results of a fitted formula or simulations. Defense 6–7 is deliberately exceptional; HP remains 2–5.

Exactly-three affordable examples: Concord Kalawi/Hester/Tavi = 14; Wildmoon Niala/Ketu/Ilex = 14. Over-budget triples remain illegal; cheap heroes create room for equipment.

Later recruits pay for both unique and regional effects and synergy; campaign PP and RP pricing are not yet finalized. No legacy linked-card charge.

Presentation, Prototype and Open Decisions

Each hero uses a half-poker portrait-orientation art/name card in the action queue, inside a faction ownership holder, plus a persistent poker-size tableau card with stats and full unique text. HP hearts sit along the portrait’s right edge. Equipment tucks under the tableau; damage cubes and statuses sit on it.

Stat notation everywhere: Attack dice (range), e.g. 4 (3), never a dash or slash before range. EXACTLY-2 attacks retain their explicit restriction. Attack/sword icon is red (#c83135); ranged-attack/bow icon green (#2b863f); Defense/shield icon blue (#2878c2), across digital plans, cards, items, tokens and legends. Heart icons use a white 3-point outline: 12.5 pixels total stroke at 300 dpi, with a visible outer half of 6.25 pixels, exceeding the requested five-pixel minimum. Digital rendering must use at least a five-pixel visible white outline at display size.

Concord: maroon angular sun. Wildmoon: sky-blue antler crescent. Raster faction masters live in assets/factions; approved original portraits in assets/heroes/approved-v9; four new full-body portraits with colored backgrounds in assets/heroes/new-starters. All visual masters remain in the game-root assets folder. Do not regenerate approved original characters.

PnP: one concise rules-and-legend sheet, no assembly cover; 10 tableau cards, 10 portrait cards, 6 faction holders, 10 enlarged hero-only standees, 2 recruit reference cards, 48 tactics, 9 distinct objectives, and 14 items (8 equipment plus 6 recruit upgrade references) on ONE sheet. No card backs and no recruit standees, including reserve recruits. Supply recruit miniatures/proxies separately. Standee faces are 1.25 × 2.5 inches, exactly 125% of the former dimensions; 10 unfolded rectangles of 1.25 × 5 inches touch edge to edge in two rows of five. The original unboxed map remains two sheets.

Tokens: 10 TOTAL each Enraged, Fortified, Dazed, Poison; four traps and five setup Brightstone with nine identical square backs; reserve Brightstone for hero defeat and VP markers. Red cubes represent 1 damage. No Taunt, Stunned, element assignment markers or linked ability cards. Printed token counts are not gameplay stack caps.

All token squares, including the nine matching trap/Brightstone token backs, touch edge to edge in one compact grid. Removing card backs does not remove these shared random-setup token backs. Recruit references show only faction symbol/name and the same HP, Move, Attack and Defense icons as heroes (2 HP, Move 2, Attack 1, Defense 1); no meeple or rules text. Print the queue as two joined three-position header strips above the portrait holders, with downward pointers only and no card-sized spaces beneath them.

Current PnP source: print-and-play/build_current.py; current output folder: print-and-play/current. This replaces the older PDF kit for the next test. Application implementation remains Sol’s work; do not infer digital completion from PnP checks.

Remaining future decisions: Highholm unique details, other later unique selections, campaign prices/progression and catch-up RP formula. TEST values still need playtesting: stats/prices, queue AP/RAP, tactic count/hand size, critical-defense reward and heavy attack. None blocks the starter test.

Loop risks to measure: card stacking and Enraged can burst down fragile heroes; repeated card-only buffs can stockpile; high Defense may stall weak attacks; hero respawn can farm stones/cards, whereas recruit defeats now yield only defeat VP; objective depletion is intentionally permanent; poison can kill before healing. Windlass later recruits require exact-two positioning to gain Enraged. Hester’s simple defensive ability is an explicit exception to the general preference for disruptive guards.
