5. Setup, Queue and Recruit Actions

Carry forward the existing deployment bidding rules: secret zone choice and nonnegative whole-PP bid; different zones ignore bids and randomly choose first side; same zone gives the higher bidder its chosen zone and first deployment/activation, with the winning bid added to the opponent’s budget. Tied bids receive secret positive increments until unequal. No bid cap.

Field exactly three distinct available heroes and three free recruits. Buy equipment, extra recruits, group upgrades and extra starting draws before seeing cards. Only unused bid compensation survives as an attacker-reroll bank; ordinary leftovers expire. Roster access does not waive fielding cost.

Starting PP is 15. Field exactly three heroes; starter prices span 3–7 PP. No inherited 20/21-PP starting budget. Later campaign stages have higher-cost heroes and higher preparation budgets; progression is OPEN.

Fire, Lightning and Water are printed elements on tactics and ability costs, never assigned hero/holder elements. A printed tactic effect needs no element match; ability payment matches its printed cost. Ownership frames and color+shape pairing identify units independently of elements.

Deploy heroes and recruits individually in the chosen zone (north blue or south stone). Never deploy or respawn into partial hexes, including overflow placement. If full, choose a nearest reachable empty hex, choosing ties. Draw a starting tactic hand and one hidden tactical objective per side. Starting hand/deck quantities are TEST below.

Only heroes have queue cards: six cards for a three-hero-per-side battle. Recruits have no queue entry. The lower bidder owns highest position 5, then ownership alternates down: lower bidder 5/3/1; higher bidder 4/2/0. Shuffle each side’s three portraits and randomly deal into its assigned positions. When equal bids are ignored because zones differ, use the second player for positions 5/3/1 as the test tie fallback; first-player selection otherwise follows existing bidding rules.

At the start of a side’s turn, resolve its scenario scoring and check victory immediately. Then each living poisoned unit owned by that side takes 1 damage and discards 1 Poison, regardless of stack size; resolve defeat and victory before selecting a hero. TEST ordering: scenario scoring precedes poison. Select any friendly hero including slot 0, capture its AP/RAP entitlement, move its card to slot 0 and shift intervening cards. Queue movement no longer triggers poison or any poison swap.

AP can be spent on the selected hero or any friendly recruit. RAP (recruit action points) can be spent only on friendly recruits. AP and RAP may combine to pay one recruit action; spend RAP first by default, with the expenditure shown. No other friendly hero gets ordinary actions.

Hero basic actions: Move, Attack, Grab, Respawn. Each distinct action has its own use count during that activation: first use costs 1 AP, second 2 AP, third 3 AP, and so on. Move–Attack–Move costs 1 + 1 + 2 AP. Paid unique abilities have their own repeat counter; base 1 AP becomes 1/2/3, base 2 AP becomes 2/3/4. Card-only abilities remain card-only on repeat use. Printed “once” means once per own activation unless another timing is stated.

Each recruit has one shared action counter for the entire friendly turn, across all its actions and regardless of AP versus RAP. Move–Attack–Move by one recruit costs 1 + 2 + 3. Another recruit begins at 1. Counters reset at the next friendly turn, not on respawn; the former once-per-action restriction is removed.

CONFIRMED six-position queue, bottom to top: 0 = 1 AP / 1 RAP; 1 = 2 AP / 1 RAP; 2 = 3 AP / 1 RAP; 3 = 3 AP / 2 RAP; 4 = 4 AP / 3 RAP; 5 = 5 AP / 2 RAP. The top step intentionally trades 1 RAP for 1 AP; neither monotonic RAP nor increasing total AP+RAP is required. All positions are selectable; no queue position draws a tactic. Slot 0 selection leaves queue order unchanged.

End activation: collect automatic Brightstone pickups and tactic rewards and enforce hand limits; evaluate/claim at most one eligible objective and resolve 1 VP + 1 tactic, checking victory immediately; if the objective hand is empty, draw one if available; resolve the activated hero’s current spreadsheet end-activation ability, if any; discard unused AP/RAP and pass. No further checkpoint occurs after victory. Regional effects follow current sheet wording and do not automatically apply to idle heroes.

6. Combat, Death and Status Resolution

Move spends its current action cost to move up to printed Move. Occupancy, board bounds and impassable terrain apply; units cannot move through units. Attack spends its current action cost and resolves one attack. Grab collects a Brightstone on the unit’s own hex immediately; both heroes and recruits may collect. Automatic end-activation collection remains available to all living friendly units standing on stones (TEST timing interpretation). Respawn spends its current action cost, returns one defeated model fully healed and status-free using deployment overflow rules, and does not reset that model’s action counters.

Push and Pull are effects of named abilities, not basic actions. For Push N, move the target up to N adjacent hex steps, each increasing hex distance from the source’s fixed position by exactly 1. For Pull N, each step reduces that distance by exactly 1. The effect’s controller chooses legal steps. Never enter occupied/impassable/out-of-bounds hexes; stop when blocked or defeated. No sideways step at equal distance, no moving through the source, no automatic damage, Dazed or opposed roll. An ability specifies any additional effects. A blocked displacement does not cancel separately printed effects.

Attack and Defense are D6 pools. Roll simultaneously and compare highest face; if both are 6, compare counts of 6. Attacker wins every remaining tie. A successful attack deals base 1 damage PLUS 1 damage for EACH attacker six kept in its final result. A successful defense grants 1 Focus for EACH defender six kept in its final result. Losing-side sixes grant no damage or Focus. These replace the two-more-sixes threshold and Fortified/Boosted defense reward. Apply damage bonuses only on a hit.

Defender and attacker alternate pre-roll tactic/pass; a play resets consecutive passes and two passes close that window. Use only the new printed tactic effects or matching-element ability payments. Effects modify the relevant participating unit, including recruits; no element match is required for a printed tactic effect. Minimum final pool is 0: roll two dice and retain only the lower as the entire result; only that one kept die can count as a six. Declare damage bonuses before rolling. A Cancel response window must finish before the targeted printed tactic effect or tactic-paid hero ability resolves.

CONFIRMED Heavy Attack (x): a hit adds x damage. This replaces the old optional heavy-attack mode entirely: no dice halving, no generic +1 heavy damage and no miss-Dazed penalty. Attacker-only rerolls remain 1 PP per own die from banked bid compensation; explicitly finish rerolls before resolution. The new counterattack tactic uses ordinary legal targeting/combat. TEST: retain the existing no-counterattack-chain rule.

Five status types exist: Focus, Enraged, Fortified, Dazed and Poison. Focus adds +1 Attack die per token to the unit’s next attack roll; spend the entire participating stack after that roll, hit or miss. Enraged adds +1 damage per token to the next attack only on a hit and is spent after that attack even on a miss. Fortified adds +1 Defense die per token; Dazed subtracts 1 Defense die per token; spend both complete participating stacks after the next defense even if they cancel numerically. Dazed does not reduce Attack.

Snapshot applicable status stacks at the roll boundary. Spend those participating stacks after resolution; newly gained after-effect tokens survive. Focus and Enraged are attack-only; Fortified and Dazed are defense-only. A successful defender’s new Focus can be used by a subsequent legal counterattack. A living hero gains 1 Enraged EACH time damage reduces it from above 1 HP to exactly 1 HP, including after healing within the same life. Remaining at 1 HP, healing to 1 HP or taking lethal damage does not trigger it. Statuses belong to units, never queue positions.

Poison (x) adds x Poison tokens on a successful hit. At the start of each owning player’s turn, each living on-board poisoned unit takes exactly 1 damage and discards exactly 1 Poison token, regardless of stack size. This applies equally to heroes and recruits. Remove poison-driven queue swaps, far-end/start-hero-activation damage, per-token burst damage, persistent stacks after a tick and instant recruit defeat. Ordinary defeat cleanup still applies.

Healing restores its normal amount of HP AND removes all Poison tokens from the target. Poison cleansing no longer replaces healing. A poisoned but unwounded unit is a legal heal target; never exceed printed HP and never revive a defeated unit through an ordinary heal.

Hero HP and other hero data are maintained in the live Battlescrap spreadsheet. Recruits have 2 HP, Move {hex:3}, Attack 2 and Defense 2 before upgrades. Damage reduces remaining HP; reaching 0 defeats the unit. Healing restores HP and also clears Poison. No generic Remove Daze or Cleanse action exists.

Each HERO defeat drops one physical Brightstone at its last hex, draws one tactic for its owner and moves its queue card to slot 0. Recruit defeats drop no stone and draw no consolation card. Secured stones never drop again. Resolve private overflow for the drawing owner even off-turn. Defeat, queue movement, rewards and immediate victory must each resolve once; queue movement has no poison side effect.

7. Map and Scenario

Each scenario uses a map plus its own deployment/spawning edges, special rules, VP sources and victory threshold or explicit VP payout. A map can support multiple scenarios and multiple group spawning edges; scenario rules specify which are active. No map-specific victory condition is inferred from artwork.

CONFIRMED Three Fires: water/north scores 1 VP at the start of each of its own turns, regardless of bonfire control. Stone/south scores 1 VP PER bonfire it controls at the start of each of its own turns. Control means more adjacent friendly units than enemy units; ties are uncontrolled. First to 15 VP wins; check immediately after every award. Carry forward 1 VP per enemy defeat and no friendly-fire VP.

Structures arrive on later maps, particularly Timberwood. They block movement but never line of sight and are destructible only by abilities that say so. Nothing blocks line of sight; do not add starter-map structures.

Random setup: every map has nine X locations. For this map shuffle four traps and five Brightstone tokens with identical backs face down onto the Xs, then reveal all before play. The boxed reference identifies positions only; print the original map without boxes.

Any movement into a trap deals 1 damage, then removes it. Any movement into fire deals 1 damage; ending a character’s turn there also deals 1 damage. Resolve each entry separately, including voluntary and forced movement, stopping if defeated. Fire hexes are enterable. TEST recruit timing: fire end-turn damage applies to recruits that acted during that friendly turn. Moving an idle hero by an ability is not itself ending its activation.

TEST structure representation: impassable occupied hexes, with adjacency measured normally. Structures have no ordinary HP/attack target status and grant no automatic cover unless a scenario or ability specifies it. Do not inherit old temporary-wall expiry or cover rules onto new structures. Preserve any explicitly authored edge-wall rules separately.

Melee flanking is +1 Attack die PER other friendly unit adjacent to the target that has no adjacent enemy OTHER than that target. Recruits can assist; the attacker cannot count itself. This is the only generic flanking bonus; ranged attacks never gain it.

Ordinary ranged attacks use printed range and may reach twice that range at −2 Attack dice, following the live spreadsheet’s updated Range rule. Any ranged attack made while at least one enemy is adjacent to the attacker suffers −2 Attack dice, once regardless of adjacent-enemy count. The two penalties stack. EXACTLY {hex:2} permits only distance {hex:2}, without extended range or Sighting Lens extension. Units never block sight. Non-attack abilities gain no combat modifiers unless printed.

Elevation remains an optional TEST: Low 0 / High 1; ranged high-to-low attacks gain +1 Attack die. No new climbing/falling/sight rules. Off unless the supplied map enables it. Poison-defeat credit for VP is OPEN; recommended: remember the enemy that applied poison and award defeat VP once to that side, without counting the poison damage as an attack for objectives.

9. Decks, Brightstone and Tactical Objectives

Both sides draw from one shared tactic draw pile and discard into one shared tactic discard pile; hands remain private. Fire, Lightning and Water remain equally represented. All old linked hero cards and the former Attack/Defense-only tactic definitions are retired. A card is discarded for either its printed effect OR one matching-element ability payment, never both.

CONFIRMED tactic effects: Cancel an opponent’s tactic immediately after it is played for its printed effect or discarded to activate a hero ability; cancellation prevents the targeted effect/ability; before an attack add 2 Attack dice; before an attack add 2 Defense dice; before an attack add 2 damage if it hits; before an attack arrange for the defender to attack immediately if the original attack misses; during your turn move one of your units up to {hex:4} OR heal it 1; during your turn attack with each friendly Recruit. Remove all generic +1 Attack, +1 Defense and +1 damage alternatives and all card-for-AP uses. Matching-element ability payments remain legal.

Ability elements are fixed in the text. A base ability normally costs one matching tactic; some powerful abilities also cost AP. An amplification discard is additional to the activation discard and any AP. Card-only abilities never acquire an AP repeat surcharge. These choices make tactic income, hand size and repeated card-only effects important balance variables.

CONFIRMED digital tactic components: one shared 27-card deck for both sides, with 9 Fire + 9 Lightning + 9 Water. Each element has two +2 Attack dice cards, two +2 hit damage cards, and one each of Cancel, +2 Defense dice, counterattack-on-miss, Move {hex:4} OR heal 1, and attack-with-each-Recruit. Totals across the shared deck: 6 +2 Attack dice, 6 +2 hit damage, and 3 of each other effect. This replaces the earlier personal-deck counts. Starting hand 4 per side and hand limit 8 remain TEST. Existing PnP files remain frozen; their old 48-card inventory is historical.

Complete each draw and enforce limit privately. When the shared tactic draw pile is empty, shuffle the shared tactic discard pile into it; if both shared piles are empty, draw nothing. Draw sources are starting hand, purchased starting draws, each Brightstone pickup, each completed objective and hero defeat. No automatic empty-tactic-hand refill is added: the empty-hand draw rule applies to OBJECTIVES.

CONFIRMED objective deck: six distinct objectives only, one copy each, replacing to01–to10. Keep one hidden starting objective per side, maximum hand 2, next-own-turn eligibility, at most one claim at friendly turn end, 1 VP + 1 tactic per claim, and draw one when the objective hand is empty and deck nonempty. Never refill or reshuffle. Use new versioned IDs so old objective progress cannot map onto new meanings.

Every completed objective gives its printed 1 VP AND one tactic card. It gives no RP, no physical stone and no additional objective draw except the empty-hand rule. Check scenario victory immediately when VP is awarded; do not run a later normal cleanup if victory ends play.

Six objectives, each 1 VP + 1 tactic: v12-to01 Close the Net — attack an enemy adjacent to at least three of your allies; v12-to02 Break the Levy — attack two different enemy recruits in one turn; v12-to03 Into the Teeth — attack an enemy with a hero adjacent to at least two enemies; v12-to04 Pin Them Down — end your turn with three friendly recruits adjacent to the same enemy; v12-to05 At Their Gates — end your turn with at least two friendly units adjacent to the enemy spawn area; v12-to06 Claim the Starfall — end your turn with all three of your heroes occupying printed X locations (Brightstone/Trap setup spaces).

“Attack” means a legally declared attack that reaches its dice roll; hitting or defeating is not required. Check adjacency immediately before that roll, after any pre-roll movement. 10. Hero Effect Semantics and Costing

Read the live Battlescrap spreadsheet for each hero’s current unique ability, cost, stats, region and availability. Document or code copies are implementation snapshots, never competing hero authority. One unique per starter and no starter regional ability remain the baseline unless the spreadsheet explicitly changes them.

All options use Fire, Lightning or Water as fixed printed elements. “Water” as a cost means discard one Water tactic, not own a Water holder. If an option says “1 AP + Water,” pay its current scaled AP cost and discard one Water tactic. Repeats of a card-only option require another matching card but no AP.

Default timing is the active hero’s own activation outside combat. Options do not create free defensive reactions. An attack granted inside an ability enters normal combat; it is not a basic Attack for equipment or basic-Attack counters. This separate-counter interpretation is TEST and can let players alternate basic and ability attacks cheaply; evaluate combined Attack counting as a possible alternative.

Movement granted by an ability does not interact with other movement/action costs or counters, following the live spreadsheet’s current rule. Do not increment a recruit’s shared paid-action counter for ability movement. Forced Push/Pull and swaps likewise do not increment counters. Multi-target abilities select distinct units and resolve one at a time; optional movement may be skipped explicitly.

Hero-specific targeting, stacking and movement exceptions belong in the spreadsheet’s current ability text and Notes. Read those fields together; if they disagree, ask the designer instead of restoring an older document ruling. Recent live edits already change Kalawi’s AP/effect and Hester’s effect.

“Within N” includes distance 0 for allies unless “another” is stated. Enemies are living on-board units. Healing can target wounded or poisoned units, restores normal HP and clears the full Poison stack. A combined heal/buff can target an unwounded unpoisoned ally only if its buff applies. Never exceed printed HP.

Taunt and Taunt tokens are removed. Future Highholm unique design and all later hero ability details belong in the live spreadsheet and remain unfinished where marked TBD.

The three-nearest-enemies abilities select all available enemies up to three within the printed radius, with the acting player choosing distance ties. They cannot skip a nearer enemy to reach a farther priority target. Resolve the chosen list from the initial positions; later movement does not retarget.

EXACTLY {hex:2} is a hard targeting condition measured when an attack/effect begins. If a chained Push fails to leave the target at distance {hex:2}, a subsequent exact-range attack cannot occur; the paid ability is not refunded. Timed effects do not stack with another copy of themselves unless stated; status tokens do stack.

Regional abilities do not belong to starters. Later recruited heroes have their region’s ability as well as their unique.

Starting PP: 15. Exactly three heroes. Starter prices: integer 3–7; the strongest package should substantially outperform the cheapest, without treating point ratio as a proved win-rate ratio.

Evaluate durability from HP and survival under opposed dice; offense from hit probability times damage over a normal activation; mobility/control from reachable targets, displacement and hazard opportunities; support from affected allies. Weight ability frequency by matching-card supply and escalating AP. Estimate synergy separately rather than pricing each printed die identically.

Hero costing remains TEST: compare full durability, offense, mobility/control and support packages, preserving half-point intermediate estimates and rounding once. The live sheet owns final printed stats and prices. Do not infer a fitted formula or proven balance.

Validate current three-hero team affordability against the live sheet and the 15-PP preparation budget. Recompute examples when hero prices change; historical triples are not fixed legality fixtures.

Later recruits pay for both unique and regional effects and synergy; campaign PP and RP pricing are not yet finalized. No legacy linked-card charge.

Regional abilities and their current wording are maintained in the Regions tab of the live Battlescrap spreadsheet: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1hfWFhxm-Su-qtR_hVrzFt6GBW1ure8W4xmp1o8hF-Rg/edit. Do not duplicate a stale regional ability catalog here.

14. Current World and Regional Themes

The designer’s refined Surlune theme below is current canon; the Design Draft’s World and Regions section remains the lore authority. Preserve its regional cultures. Seven regions: Windlands, Cinder’s Rim, Port Windlass, Wellington and Phalexia in starter offers; Highholm and Timberwood later. Region is not allegiance.

Concord’s maroon sun and Wildmoon’s sky-blue antler crescent indicate current ownership; small starter-faction reminders indicate initial assignment. Pair color+shape connects a queue hero to its board piece. Background palettes express identity/region. Preserve approved art under the game-root assets folder.

One day, Surlune’s stars began to fall.

Not all at once, not all together. But they stormed through the land, some sprinkling as hot, white glowing pebbles that streamed down to the earth, others as massive streaking boulders crashing through the atmosphere and carving new mountain valleys or splintering across farms and towns.

But the stones, Brightstones as they came to be called, didn’t just destroy, they brought a will with them. People who witnessed that first crash saw terrible miracles. Where the stones had landed in a forest, some trees rose to the heavens while others started to move with purpose, smashing nearby lumberyards. Rivers flooded and swelled with life, deserts saw their sand turned to tiny gemstones, blinding in the light and home to crystal-scaled lizards the size of a horse. And the horses? And all the other creatures that found and got too close to, or simply lived near these Brightstones long enough? Like nothing you recognize as sane now.

People were warped too. Strange tales come from the furthest reaches of civilization still, but over time people learned to work with Brightstone. A farmer might find them bringing a more bountiful harvest, a carpenter’s poor cut might mend. Towns quickly became cities, and cities look to become kingdoms.

When a group of high-minded soldiers formed what they called a Concord (and they insist it had nothing to do with the wine-producing valley they came from) with neighboring cities to band together against the rampant theivery and other dangers on the road, it didn’t take long for that protection to be extended to neighboring areas that had not asked for it. Brightstone was power, and power was needed to fight back.

As the Concord grew, the many moon-worshiping Lunites saw a future in the nacent empire that they didn’t want for their homes or families. As word passed through the moonlit gatherings of the many villages dotting the land, talk of resisting the Concord grew. It’s not clear who started calling this resistance the “Wildmoon”, but the name stuck and only served to bind people to it’s purpose: an end to the Concord, a future where Brightstone is for everyone.

The players are Stonebinders, they have a special gift for drawing great power from Brightstone, healing grievous wounds and seeing their surroundings from the astral plane where they can suggest, or even command, those around them. Stonebinders make natural commanders, and this game is the story of some of them fighting for a better future on Surlune.
