# Galax playtest rulings — 2026-09-21

The earlier scaffold decisions are superseded by this source-backed rebuild. The following interpretations are provisional; source conflicts were raised with the designer, but no answer had arrived at implementation time.

1. **Source precedence:** September 20 authored reference faces and the design document's Current Rules override conflicting older rulebook text. Speculative To Try / older playtest notes are excluded. This is not a rules-complete claim.
2. **Research:** use the printed 0–12 track. Level 4 gives +1 attacking shield, 6 gives +1 trophy for exploiting enemy Civs, 8 gives +1 battle power, crossing 10 draws five cards up to the hand limit, and 12 scores 3 VP. Blue civilized systems contribute +3 effective Research each, lost when their last Civ disappears. Technologies use per-turn rank capacity, not permanent capacity consumption. Research and civilization benefits are recomputed live.
3. **Setup:** use the older rulebook's four-point deployment budget and six starting hand cards. A home is chosen from that hand. Current unnumbered planets use resistance 1 for deployment. If a hand contains no system at all, exchange one anomaly for a system from the deck. The three-player map preserves the diagram's three exposed positions; their contents are randomly selected systems rather than fixed example artwork.
4. **Construction:** pay 2 Commerce AP per chosen planet (1 when exploited), plus enemy-fleet interference once for the activation. Any affordable subset can be chosen. Subsurface Development allows a second Civ on an already-owned planet with a base cost of 2, subject to cost reductions. Minimum cost is 1 per Civ. Building consumes that system's Commerce activation; the newer reference omits the old rulebook's automatic free follow-up activation, so none is granted.
5. **Exploitation:** enemy fleets block it. Pay 1 Explore AP; choose a number from 1–5, reveal a deck card, and receive that amount of the planet's resource when the guess is at most the revealed rank. A normal planet has no resource, but can still be marked. Empty planets receive an Exploit token even on a failed guess; enemy Civs stay and award trophies. Full marker supply reuses a farthest marked planet by map distance. Anti Space Guns casualties are paid before exploitation resolves.
6. **Conquer:** pay 1 Military AP plus enemy-fleet interference, play one hand card or the permanent zero, then compare total fleet/Red-system/technology power plus that card's rank against a deck card. Ties succeed. Success converts a selected fleet into a Civ, replaces opponent Civs and removes exploitation. Failure retains the fleet; the later note proposing a spent fleet on failure was outside Current Rules. The old planet resistance comparison is superseded. Propaganda Net uses a free power-versus-deck attempt and supplies a new Civ without consuming its II fleet.
7. **Anti Space Guns c40:** malformed “discard a fleet or per Civ” means discard one fleet per defending Civ at the affected system. The attacker chooses casualties. If all attacking fleets are lost, the action cannot proceed.
8. **Tech Override c18:** “a total of exactly 4” means discard hand cards whose ranks sum to exactly 4, or discard the targeted fleet.
9. **The Gorb c29:** capture an enemy battling fleet under a geographically nearest friendly Civ; ties let its owner choose the prison. A released fleet uses an available waypoint at that system; if none exists it returns to supply. Its owner receives the printed release trophy. Capture and casualty ordering are explicit in the effect queue.
10. **Battle:** ties favor the attacker. Power is fixed before burst casualties, as the rulebook describes. The zero card is exclusive and grants a shield only when defending. Self-Evolving Robots exposes one card before commitment; both robots trigger when both players possess the effect. Full-Spectrum Holography adds one normal card slot. Retreat uses normal movement; an armada with no exit loses an additional fleet and any survivors remain.
11. **Anomalies:** reference faces grant asteroid bonuses to adjacent draws/builds, nebula bonuses to adjacent research, nebula battle-card trophies, and wormhole movement. Spacetime Deflection grants all *system benefits* for a civilized anomaly; it does not supply all four colors for a Culture victory.
12. **Automatic choices:** Nanodistributed Compute upgrades the first eligible level-I fleet per armada; physically identical choices use stable token IDs. Dyson Terraformer preserves Civs in stable order across replacement planet slots and returns overflow to supply. These are convenience rulings to revisit if specific token identity matters.
13. **End game:** Super Nova is inserted into the bottom half at the first/second/third reshuffle for 2/3/4 players. Drawing/discarding it ends the current turn, not an arbitrary turn counter. A numeric flip revealing Super Nova has value 0 while scheduling the end. Culture, Hegemony and Unification Theory have explicit victory paths; final ties use summed hand rank, then shared victory.
14. **Privacy and undo:** native hotseat handoffs hide private cards and isolate keyboard focus. Undo and full replay are cooperative playtest tools that can reveal history; exports say so explicitly. No multiplayer networking is implied.
15. **Statistics:** starting-color preference rotates across simulation seeds/seats but cannot override which colors were dealt. Report sample counts and avoid interpreting these conditional samples or technology-user outcomes as causal balance evidence. Record plays for every technology/discovery; activation counters cover explicit effect hooks, not all continuous prevention effects.
